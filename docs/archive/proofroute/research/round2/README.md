## What changed because of the research

1. **The generic headline failed the prior-work screen.** Stood/Rentabot/AgentEscrow already disclose frozen acceptance-gated payments. Routific already offers AI dispatch. VROOM audits supplied routes; Timefold replans; RouteNexa has a what-if simulator. Those are substantive overlaps, not footnotes. The product now leads with a precise merchant promise failure and one accepted rescue, without claiming any ingredient is new.
2. **The demo is falsifiable.** The synthetic A,B,C plan is 4000m and nominally valid but B starts900s late under CORRIDOR_DELAY. B,A,C is 4400m and the only permutation passing all 3 under 4500m. The route order is shared; separate scenario schedules are independently recomputed. Root arithmetic also yields300s minimum window margin,10:25 worst return and150s uniform-extra-leg tolerance. These are static oracle checks, not an executed solver/app.
3. **Payment semantics became more precise.** Freeze the work deadline at the first authorization send; never let a recovered response restart it. Separate untrusted webhook receipt from authenticated event dedup. Preserve uncertain capture lanes, actual resource statuses, partial adjustments, request-ID documentation conflicts and session-lock limits.
4. **Company alignment became concrete.** Recent public work emphasizes identity-preserving execution, reviewed/versioned workflows, authoritative structured context and traceable evidence. Apply those principles inside the merchant workflow. Do not duplicate PayPal's internal MCP gateway/skills registry or infer private employee/hiring priorities.
5. **The build is extensive but deadline-aware.** Full product70–100 focused hours; flagship 44–58 including reserve; base safe path 32–38. At Nov10 14:55IST about58h35m remained until Nov13 01:30IST. Scope cuts are required if the real vertical slice slips. Research does not make that implementation risk disappear.

## Why this choice survives the challenge

The product offers a legible trigger and output: an operator has a plan; a reviewed customer promise fails under an explicit assumption; a paid rescue passes those assumptions and comes with useful schedules. Its engineering depth is inspectable in the constraint model, independent checker, evidence lineage and recoverable external effects. Actual AI translates merchant intent before consent; PayPal handles a genuine service purchase rather than a decorative button.

The commercial objection is real: capable routing tools have free tiers, and a merchant may not buy a separate assurance layer. The price450 cents is a demo hypothesis. The proposed wedge is explicit source-to-promise evidence plus finite-scenario acceptance for occasional/agent-mediated procurement; willingness to pay has not been validated. Post-hackathon, test whether operators change a decision after seeing an exact counterexample and rescue report. Do not claim a moat, paid demand or mileage savings from this research.

The alternatives screen found no stronger evidence-backed pivot within the deadline: catalog-readiness repair overlaps existing bots/platforms and gated Store Sync; reliability rescue overlaps Unhappy Path/Codebusters/PayPal integration agents; staffing assurance adds unfamiliar semantics without stronger demand evidence. Staying with dispatch is a judgment about a concrete demonstrable workflow, not proof it is the globally optimal idea.
